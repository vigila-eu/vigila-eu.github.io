What Vigila promises — and what it can't.

Effective: 4 August 2026 · Applies to: Vigila for iOS & watchOS, the Vigila relay, and the Vigila push bridge · Česká verze

the short version

Vigila does its best to tell you when something breaks, but alert delivery crosses hardware and services nobody controls end to end — your routers, your network, Apple's push service, your phone. Treat Vigila as a helpful early warning, not as a guarantee, and never as the only thing standing between you and a serious failure.

1. What Vigila is

Vigila monitors network devices running RouterOS and notifies you when a rule you configured is triggered. It is a monitoring aid for people who administer their own networks. You are responsible for the devices you monitor, the credentials you give the app, and the rules you configure.

2. Alert delivery is best-effort

This is the most important thing on this page. An alert reaching your phone depends on a chain in which we operate exactly one link:

  • your routers being reachable and answering correctly,
  • your own network and internet connection,
  • your self-hosted relay, if you run one — its host, its power, its uptime,
  • the Vigila bridge, which forwards encrypted alerts,
  • Apple Push Notification service, and
  • your device — its connectivity, battery, notification settings, and the way iOS schedules background work.

We do not guarantee that any particular alert will be delivered, or delivered within any particular time. A failure anywhere in that chain can delay an alert or prevent it entirely. Your relay retains undelivered alerts and retries them for a bounded period, which turns most short interruptions into late alerts rather than missing ones — but that is a mitigation, not a promise.

3. Not for safety-critical use

Vigila is not designed, tested, or certified for use where a missed or delayed alert could cause injury, loss of life, environmental harm, or significant property damage. Do not rely on it for medical, life-support, emergency-response, industrial control, or similar purposes. If an outage in what you monitor carries consequences of that kind, use monitoring built and warranted for it, with redundancy that does not share Vigila's delivery path.

4. Availability

The Vigila bridge is a hosted service offered without an uptime commitment. We maintain backups, monitoring and a documented recovery procedure, and we will restore service as promptly as we reasonably can — but there is no service level agreement, and we may take the service down for maintenance. The rest of Vigila runs on hardware you own and is unaffected by our availability, apart from push delivery.

5. Your responsibilities

  • Keep your devices, your relay and its host reasonably secure and up to date.
  • Only monitor and control devices you are authorised to administer.
  • Enabling "Allow control" lets Vigila enable or disable network interfaces on that router — the only write operation it performs, and off by default. It changes nothing else, but switching an interface off is a real change to a real network, and you own the consequences.
  • Verify that alerts actually reach you after you set them up, and re-verify after changing devices, reinstalling, or changing notification settings.
